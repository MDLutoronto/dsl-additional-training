See which texts are available
-----------------------------

([back to table of contents](#table_of_contents))

To see which collections of primary sources are available for your research, follow these steps:

1. Return to the DSL main page by clicking on the *Digital Scholar Lab* logo.  
<img src='{{ '/assets/images/DSL_Training01_001.png' | relative_url }}' alt='Click on the logo to back to main page' title='' width='577' height='127' />
2. Scroll down until you see the "Support for Your Research and Learning Needs" title.  
<img src='{{ '/assets/images/DSL_Training01_002.png' | relative_url }}' alt='Interface for extra support' title='' width='590' height='416' />
3. Under "What Texts are Available?", click See the available archives.  
<img src='{{ '/assets/images/DSL_Training01_003.png' | relative_url }}' alt='View available archives' title='' width='578' height='434' />
4. You will be taken to a screen with a complete listing of all *Gale Primary Source* collections licensed to the University of Toronto. You can then filter specific items or search within specific collections.<img src='{{ '/assets/images/Additional-4.jpg' | relative_url }}' alt='' title='' width='1209' height='659' />
5. Gale adds individual Learning Centers to many of these primary source collections. If a particularly collection interests you, click on it and check if there is a Learning Center available. Each Learning Center includes information about the collection and tips for using it, arranged in four categories:
	1. **Conceptualize:**for information about the collection as a whole, its archival source(s), sample research questions, and content advisories
	2. **Find:**Search strategies including advanced search options (e.g. Boolean operators, proximity to other terms), collection\-specific key terms, browsing the archive, and using text analysis tools
	3. **Read:**search terms, metadata, thinking critically about the collection's materials, and examples of research including lesson plans
	4. **Use:**guides to citation, copyright, and reuse, as well as research essays using the collection written by subject matter experts like researchers and librarians

DSL Learning Center
-------------------

([back to table of contents](#table_of_contents))

To access the *Digital Scholar Lab*'s Learning Center (distinct from the collection\-specific learning centers discussed above), which includes additional documentation and videos, follow these steps:

1. Return to the DSL main page by clicking on the Digital Scholar Lab logo.
2. Scroll down until you see the "Support for Your Research and Learning Needs" title.
3. Under "Dive Deeper Into How the Lab Works", click on Visit the Learning Center.  
<img src='{{ '/assets/images/DSL_Training02_001.png' | relative_url }}' alt='Visit the Learning Center button' title='' width='617' height='472' />
4. You will be taken to a new page with a sidebar menu. In addition to being able to read documentation and watch videos on any step of using the Digital Scholar Lab, you will also be able to access the Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ), Glossary, User Guidelines, and the Privacy Policy.  
<img src='{{ '/assets/images/Additional2-4.jpg' | relative_url }}' alt='' title='' width='1225' height='801' />

 

Sample Projects
---------------

([back to table of contents](#table_of_contents))

Gale's staff, which include a number of professors engaged in digital humanities research projects, created three sample projects for you to use. They provide pre\-built collections and pre\-run tools centred on specific themes. They also provide extensive documentation on how they constructed their projects, how they fine\-tuned their cleaning configurations, and how to interpret their results, which can in turn help you develop your own projects.

1. Return to the DSL main page by clicking on the Digital Scholar Lab logo.
2. Scroll down until you see the "Support for Your Research and Learning Needs" title.
3. Under "Try Out Sample Projects", click View Sample Projects.  
<img src='{{ '/assets/images/DSL_Training03_001.png' | relative_url }}' alt='View Sample Projects' title='' width='661' height='499' />
4. There are currently three projects. (Scroll down if you can't see all of them.) Click on the title of one that interests you.  
<img src='{{ '/assets/images/DSL_Training03_002.png' | relative_url }}' alt='"Black America" project selected' title='' width='869' height='332' />
5. Once you are on the page for a specific project, you have three options:

    1. Click on "Get a copy" to copy the whole project, collections, cleaning configurations, and pre\-run tools, to your account.  
        <img src='{{ '/assets/images/DSL_Training03_003.png' | relative_url }}' alt='Under project title and description, the Get a Copy button' title='' width='694' height='346' />
        2. Click on each of the entries to see an overview of how Gale's staff created this project, step by step.  
        <img src='{{ '/assets/images/DSL_Training03_004.png' | relative_url }}' alt='Menu bar for the project' title='' width='649' height='338' />
        3. Click on the "Thinking Critically Supplement PDF" to read the report providing greater detail on how the project was created, how to take it further, and what obstacles the staff ran into.  
        <img src='{{ '/assets/images/DSL_Training03_005.png' | relative_url }}' alt='Click on Thinking Critically Supplement PDF' title='' width='588' height='300' />

Use Python via Jupyter Notebooks
--------------------------------

([back to table of contents](#table_of_contents))

Although the Gale Digital Scholar Lab is designed to work without any knowledge of programming skills on your part, if you do have experience using the Python programming language, Gale supplies three Jupyter notebooks. Note that you may need, at a minimum, to modify the Python code to point to the location of your data. These notebooks work best for people with at least a basic knowledge of Python. We provide [introductory resources for learning Python](https://mdl.library.utoronto.ca/technology/tutorials/python-information-tutorials-and-workshops).

To access these notebooks, follow these instructions:

1. From the DSL homepage, scroll down. Under "Learning Center", look under the heading "WHat Analyses Can I run?". C;ocl on "Learn more".  
<img src='{{ '/assets/images/image_17.png' | relative_url }}' alt='Learning center section, with first of three "learn more" buttons highlighted' title='' width='1675' height='429' />
2. On the left "Learning Center Menu", click "Python Notebooks". (If it is not visible, expand "Analyze".)  
<img src='{{ '/assets/images/image_24.png' | relative_url }}' alt='Learning Center Menu, Python Notebooks under Analyze heading' title='' width='449' height='628' />
3. This page provide three Jupyter notebooks: Named Entity Recognition, Geographic Information System (GIS), and Sentiment Analysis. GIS is only available via these notebooks, whereas the other two tools are also available in the DSL's Analysis tools. In some cases, Python enables you to work with more advanced settings than the DSL's analysis tools. **Click on any of the "Get a copy" buttons to download a .ipynb file.** 
<img src='{{ '/assets/images/image_26.png' | relative_url }}' alt='Highlighted download links for the three jupyter notebooks' title='' width='725' height='828' />
4. You can open this file in several systems, including on your computer locally or in a browser\-based platform.
